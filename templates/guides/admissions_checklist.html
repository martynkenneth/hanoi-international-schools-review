30-second summary: Apply 9–12 months before your intended start. Popular year groups fill fast. Get two teacher references, 2 years of school reports, and passports ready first. Apply to 3–4 schools simultaneously, not just your first choice.

12-month admissions timeline

  • 12 months before start: Research schools, attend open days, narrow shortlist to 3–4. Start requesting school reports from current school.
  • 10–11 months before: Attend school open houses (most held October–November). Request teacher reference letters. Confirm which year group your child will enter.
  • 9–10 months before: Submit applications. Priority deadlines at BIS, Brighton, Rugby are typically October–January. Don't wait until spring.
  • 8–9 months before: Book and attend assessment/taster days. Prepare for any entrance exams. Scholarship assessment windows are often January–February.
  • 6–7 months before: Receive offers. If waitlisted, contact schools to confirm your position. Decline unwanted offers promptly to free places for others.
  • 5–6 months before: Pay enrollment deposit to confirm place. Ask about transport routes, uniform requirements, and induction schedule.
  • 3–4 months before: Order uniforms. Arrange transport. Complete all medical and vaccination forms. Begin any language preparation your child needs.
  • 1 month before: Attend new student orientation. Meet key staff. Ensure your child has met one or two classmates before the first day.
  • First week: Check in with class teacher daily. Watch for social settling. Remind your child that everyone is a little nervous at the start.

How do entrance assessments work at Hanoi international schools?

Most Hanoi international schools use some form of assessment to place students in the correct year group and identify any support needs — not to exclude children.

  • Primary entry (Year 1–5): Usually informal — a classroom visit or play-based assessment. Schools look for readiness, not academic excellence.
  • Year 6–7 (secondary transition): More formal English and Maths assessment. This is a more competitive entry point at popular schools.
  • Year 9–10: Academic assessment plus review of IGCSE subject choices. Schools check alignment with current curriculum.
  • Year 12 (IB / A-Level entry): Strong academic record required. Some schools conduct subject-specific assessments or interviews. Scholarship assessment is typically separate.
  • EAL assessment: If your child's first language is not English, expect an English language assessment at all entry points.

How to prepare for school visits and interviews

School visits are a two-way assessment — the school is evaluating your child, and you are evaluating the school. Prepare for both.

Questions to ask the school

  • What is the current class size in my child's year group?
  • How many teachers teach the core subjects (Maths, English, Science)?
  • How does the school support students joining mid-year?
  • What EAL / language support is available, and at what cost?
  • What is the staff turnover rate?
  • What is the school's approach to managing homework load?
  • What are the most recent IB / IGCSE / AP results?
  • Can we speak to any current parents?

Prepare your child to

  • Talk about their favourite subjects and why they enjoy them
  • Describe a project or achievement they are proud of
  • Explain what hobbies or activities they enjoy outside school
  • Ask one or two genuine questions about the school

What if my child is waitlisted?

Waitlists are common at popular schools (especially UNIS, BIS) for Year 6–7 entry. Being waitlisted is not a rejection — it means the school wants your child but doesn't have space right now.

  • Confirm your position on the waitlist in writing, and ask whether you are ranked or unranked.
  • Accept an offer from your second-choice school while you wait — places do open up, especially in July and August as families confirm relocations.
  • Maintain contact with admissions every 3–4 weeks without being pushy. A brief, polite update email is appropriate.
  • Strengthen your child's file — if additional assessments or reports are available, offer them.
  • Be ready to move quickly — when a place opens, it may be offered with a 48–72 hour response window.

FAQs about international school admissions in Hanoi

How early should I apply for a primary school place?

For the most popular primary year groups (Year 1 and Year 3), apply 9–12 months in advance. Less popular year groups may have space on shorter notice. For UNIS and BIS, waitlists for some primary years can stretch to 12–18 months.

Does my child need to speak English before applying?

Most Hanoi international schools have EAL programmes for non-native English speakers. You don't need to wait until your child is fluent — but be transparent about language level during the application process so the school can plan appropriate support.

Can I apply to multiple schools at the same time?

Yes — and you should. Apply to 3–4 schools simultaneously rather than waiting for one result before applying elsewhere. You can decline offers you don't intend to take up.

What happens if we arrive in Hanoi mid-year?

All major Hanoi international schools accept mid-year applications. Contact admissions directly with your intended arrival date. Availability is space-dependent, but most schools maintain rolling admissions with relatively fast turnaround (1–2 weeks for an initial assessment).

How can I improve my child's chances of a scholarship?

Scholarships are competitive. Prepare strong teacher references that speak to specific academic or co-curricular strengths. Brief your child on what the school values (academic, sport, music, or all-round). For Year 12 scholarships, prior IB/IGCSE predicted or actual results are the primary factor.
