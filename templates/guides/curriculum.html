What is the IB and is it right for my child?

The International Baccalaureate (IB) is a Swiss-based non-profit offering three programmes: the Primary Years Programme (PYP, ages 3–12), the Middle Years Programme (MYP, 11–16), and the Diploma Programme (DP, 16–18).

✓ IB advantages

  • Recognised by universities in 160+ countries
  • Develops critical thinking, research, and independent learning
  • Extended Essay and CAS components strengthen university applications
  • Best single framework for globally mobile families
  • Theory of Knowledge (TOK) develops genuine intellectual rigour

✗ IB considerations

  • Breadth over depth — students study 6 subjects simultaneously
  • Some UK universities prefer A-Level specialist depth
  • Diploma pass rate ~80% globally — not guaranteed
  • Premium fees at IB-authorised schools
Best fit: Internationally mobile families, children who enjoy cross-disciplinary learning, students targeting UK, US, Canadian, or Australian universities.

What is the British curriculum (IGCSE and A-Levels)?

The British curriculum runs from EYFS (Early Years) through Key Stage 1–4 (ages 4–16), culminating in IGCSE examinations at 16. Students then choose A-Levels (2–3 subjects studied in depth over 2 years) or switch to the IB Diploma. Some schools offer A-Levels only; others (BIS Hanoi) offer a choice.

✓ British curriculum advantages

  • A-Levels: deep specialist knowledge valued by UK universities
  • IGCSE widely recognised — 160+ countries accept it
  • Structured, rigorous, internationally portable
  • UK Ofsted inspection standards provide quality assurance

✗ British curriculum considerations

  • A-Levels studied in only 2–3 subjects — narrow for US college applications
  • Less emphasis on community service and research vs IB
  • UK-brand school fees among the highest in Hanoi
Best fit: Families targeting UK university entry, children who prefer depth over breadth, families with a UK educational background.

What is the American curriculum (AP)?

US-curriculum schools follow Common Core state standards through Grade 11, then offer Advanced Placement (AP) courses in Grade 11–12. Each AP exam (scored 1–5) can earn college credit at US universities. Students graduate with a US High School Diploma.

✓ AP curriculum advantages

  • AP scores directly recognised for college credit at 4,000+ US institutions
  • Wide course choice — up to 18+ AP subjects at some Hanoi schools
  • Project-based learning develops practical skills
  • Competitive fees at some Hanoi AP schools

✗ AP curriculum considerations

  • Less recognised than IB or A-Level by UK and European universities
  • Some Hanoi AP schools not fully WASC accredited
  • Less portable globally than IB
Best fit: Families targeting US college applications, students who enjoy a broad course selection, families planning to return to the United States.

What is the French Baccalauréat?

The French Bac is offered exclusively at Lycée Alexandre Yersin, Hanoi. It follows the French Ministry of Education curriculum from age 3 through to the Baccalauréat at 18. The senior programme at Yersin also offers an IB Diploma track for non-French pathways.

Best fit: French national families, families with French language fluency, children targeting French universities or grandes écoles. The French Bac is widely recognised by European, UK, and North American universities.

Which curriculum leads to the best university outcomes?

All major international curricula lead to strong university outcomes — the bigger factors are the specific school, the student's own achievement, and how well the curriculum aligns with target universities.

  • Targeting UK Russell Group / Oxbridge: A-Levels → strongest. IB Diploma → very well received. AP → possible but less common.
  • Targeting Ivy League / top US colleges: IB Diploma → excellent. AP → strong. A-Levels → accepted.
  • Targeting Canadian universities: Canadian diploma (BC/Ontario) → optimal. IB → strong.
  • Targeting Australian universities: Australian curriculum → optimal. IB → strong.
  • Targeting French universities / grandes écoles: French Baccalauréat → required for most French institutions.
  • Maximising global flexibility: IB Diploma → best portfolio for applying to multiple countries simultaneously.

Frequently asked questions about Hanoi school curricula

Is the IB better than A-Levels?

Neither is objectively better. IB suits holistic learners applying globally; A-Levels provide specialist depth valued by UK universities. The "better" choice depends on your child's learning style, strengths, and target universities.

Which curriculum is best for US university applications from Hanoi?

AP is the most direct US pathway — each AP exam can earn college credit. IB Diploma is also highly regarded by US admissions officers. A-Levels are accepted but less standard in US applications.

Can a child switch curriculum mid-way through school?

Switching is possible but disruptive, especially after Grade 9. The easiest transitions: IB PYP → MYP → DP (all IB), Cambridge Primary → IGCSE, or A-Level → IB Diploma at Year 12. Switching from French Bac to IB in senior years is harder and requires language support.

Do Vietnamese universities accept IB or A-Level results?

Top Vietnamese universities (VNU, NEU, RMIT Vietnam) accept IB Diploma and A-Level results for international student admissions. Domestic Vietnamese students typically need the Vietnamese national exam (THPT). Check each university's specific admission requirements.

What if my child has been following one curriculum and we move to Hanoi mid-year?

Most Hanoi international schools are experienced with mid-year transitions. Choose a school offering the same or similar curriculum to minimise disruption. IB schools, Cambridge schools, and British curriculum schools all have structured transition support.
